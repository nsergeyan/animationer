## 0. PRIORITY

If two instructions collide, the earlier section wins. Section 1 beats
everything, always, with no exception. Never break Section 1 to satisfy a
craft note further down.

Work in this order and do not skip:

1. Fill in the whole `plan` object.
2. Write the LAST beat and put it in `plan.payoff_line`.
3. Re-read `plan.facts_to_check`. Those facts constrain the beats you are
   about to write, they are not a disclaimer you file and forget.
4. Write beats 1 to N in order, inside the ranges the plan assigns.
5. Run Section 10 by LOOKING at each beat, not by remembering it.
6. Output one raw JSON object. No markdown fences, no commentary, nothing else.

### SATISFY THE REASON, NOT THE WORDING

Every rule in this file exists because something specific came out wrong. A rule
can be obeyed to the letter and still fail the thing it was written for.

    RULE: "introduce Nifan by name in the first sentence"
    OBEYED, AND USELESS: "Nifan is a Survey Corps soldier."
    The rule got its name. The video got a label instead of a hook.

Where this file says "where possible", "when useful" or "not every one", read it
as the strong version. Those phrases are there for the rare genuine exception,
not as permission to skip the work.

When you are about to satisfy a rule in its cheapest form, that is the signal
you have found the beat worth spending real effort on.

## 1. THE NAMING LAW

This is the rule the pipeline actually enforces. Get it wrong and the run
breaks. It has exactly two halves and they are opposites. Read both.

### NARRATION: always "Nifan". Never "the reference character".

Nifan is the channel's main character and the person the story happens to.
The narration names him.

    RIGHT:  "Nifan reaches the gate before the alarm starts."
    WRONG:  "The reference character reaches the gate."
    WRONG:  "You reach the gate before the alarm starts."

Never use "you" for Nifan. Never use "the reference character" in narration,
in `plan`, or in any other text field. Name him naturally: use "Nifan" wherever
there is any ambiguity about who acted, and a pronoun where there is not.

### IMAGE PROMPTS: always "the reference character". Never "Nifan".

Both `image_prompt` and `image_prompt_alt` must contain the exact lowercase
string:

    the reference character

The image generator has Nifan's reference art attached as a picture. It has
never heard the name "Nifan" and cannot draw a name. "the reference character"
is the phrase that points at the attached art.

    RIGHT:  "the reference character leaning against the barracks wall..."
    WRONG:  "Nifan leaning against the barracks wall..."

### AND NEVER DESCRIBE HIM

The attached art already defines how he looks. Re-describing him competes with
it and measurably lowers consistency. In image prompts, never write his face,
head, hair, skin, clothes, expression, mood, age or build.

Only ever write what his BODY is DOING: posture, movement, and his physical
relationship to objects.

    RIGHT:  "the reference character crouching beside a split wagon wheel"
    WRONG:  "the reference character, a tired man with messy black hair"
    WRONG:  "the reference character looking exhausted"

### SUMMARY TABLE

    field                | main character is called   | described visually
    ---------------------+----------------------------+-------------------
    narration            | Nifan                      | never
    plan (all fields)    | Nifan                      | never
    image_prompt         | the reference character    | never
    image_prompt_alt     | the reference character    | never

Every other character in the story is the opposite: named in narration if they
have a name, and fully described visually in every single image prompt.

## 2. HARD RULES

These are countable. Count them before you output.

1.  EXACTLY the number in BEATS. Not close. Count the array.

2.  Section 1 holds in every beat with no exception.

3.  FOUR to SIX locations. Each held for six or more consecutive beats.
    Ranges cover every beat with no gaps and no overlaps. Every beat's
    `location` string matches a name in `plan.locations` character for
    character.

4.  Narration: 17 to 21 words per beat, aiming at 19. No sentence over
    fifteen words. Emotion tags do not count toward the word budget.

5.  No quotation marks anywhere. Nobody is ever quoted. People still talk,
    but the narrator reports what was said.

6.  One beat in three opens with a sentence of five words or fewer.

7.  One beat in three carries an emotion tag from the Section 6 list.
    Nothing outside that list.

8.  Every image_prompt anchors the place with visible objects and materials.
    NEVER with a date, a century, an era name or a real-world period.

8a. Nothing visible in the frame is left as a bare noun. Every person, group,
    building, uniform, vehicle, creature and environment gets a full physical
    description. See Section 7.

9.  `image_prompt_alt` exists on every beat and is a genuinely different
    staging. Never a copy, never a paraphrase, never the same words reordered.

10. No two beats share an `image_prompt`.

11. No hands doing fine work. No text or writing of any kind in an image.
    No lighting, weather or render words. Banned lists in Section 7.

12. Never write a STYLE block, an art style, a medium or an aspect ratio into
    an image prompt. The pipeline appends a fixed style to every prompt. Yours
    would compete with it and get stripped.

13. Plain ASCII inside every string. No markdown, no bullets, no curly quotes,
    no em dashes, no line breaks inside a string.

14. Never mention the video, the viewer, the channel, subscribing, liking, or
    "as we saw". There is no outro and no call to action: the pipeline bolts
    a fixed outro on afterwards.

15. BUT or THEREFORE between every pair of beats. If the only word that fits
    is AND THEN, one of the two beats is not doing anything. Rewrite it.

16. Nifan never gains a power, a weapon or a talent. His whole edge is being
    ordinary: he knows the street, nobody watches him, nobody stops him.

## 3. OUTPUT SHAPE

One raw JSON object, exactly these keys:

{
  "topic": "short slug-like title",
  "format": "what-if",
  "plan": {
    "spine_question": "the one question the video answers, in plain words",
    "setting_anchor": "six or seven words carried into every image prompt",
    "payoff_line": "the final beat's narration, written before the others",
    "final_choice": {
      "beat": Number of BEATS,
      "chooses_between": "two things Nifan wants, both of which cost him",
      "what_it_costs": "something visible: a person, an object, a place"
    },
    "locations": [
      {
        "name": "",
        "beats": "1-9",
        "why_he_is_here": "",
        "visual_anchor": ""
      }
    ],
    "sections": [
      { "beats": "1-9", "step": "YOU+NEED", "job": "" }
    ],
    "facts_to_check": [
      { "claim": "", "source": "" }
    ]
  },
  "music_prompt": "one paragraph, see Section 9",
  "beats": [
    {
      "location": "",
      "narration": "",
      "image_prompt": "",
      "image_prompt_alt": ""
    }
  ]
}

Only `location`, `narration`, `image_prompt` and `image_prompt_alt` reach the
pipeline. The `plan` exists to hold the constraints in working memory while you
write the beats. Fill it anyway, and fill it FIRST.

`setting_anchor` and each `visual_anchor` are strings you will paste verbatim
into image prompts, so write them as prompt fragments, not as sentences.

Write both from visible materials, shapes and colours only. No dates, no era
names, and nothing that cannot be seen in the frame. These two strings go into
every single beat, so a bad one is not one bad picture, it is the whole video.

    BAD:  "nineteenth century walled city besieged by giants"
          (a date, plus a fact invisible from indoors)
    GOOD: "plaster and timber town under a vast grey wall"

THE TWO ANCHORS HAVE DIFFERENT JOBS AND MUST NOT OVERLAP.

`visual_anchor` owns the location: its buildings, surfaces, materials, colours
and furniture. It changes when the location changes.

`setting_anchor` owns only what is true in EVERY location of this video, the one
or two things that say which world this is from any angle. It never changes.

Because both strings land in the same prompt, `setting_anchor` must not name a
building material, a wall surface, a roof or a ground surface that any
`visual_anchor` also names. Two answers to the same question is worse than one,
and the generator gets the contradiction in all N prompts.

    BAD:  setting_anchor "brick town inside a high grey wall"
          visual_anchor  "pale plaster houses with dark timber beams"
          (brick or plaster? every prompt asks the generator to decide)

    GOOD: setting_anchor "a vast grey wall standing many times house height"
          visual_anchor  "pale plaster houses with dark timber beams"
          (the anchor names the world, the location names the buildings)

Also: name the scale of the thing rather than an adjective for it. "high wall"
is any wall. "many times house height" is the wall this world is about.

## 4. BEAT ONE, AND THE FIRST THREE

Beat one must introduce Nifan by name in its FIRST sentence, and that sentence
must also carry a FACT. Naming him is not the job, it is the minimum. The
wording is yours and should differ from video to video.

Banned: the bare "Nifan is a X" form. It spends the most valuable sentence in
the whole video on a label.

    WEAK:   "Nifan is a Survey Corps soldier."
    STRONG: "Nifan joined the Survey Corps eleven days ago."
    STRONG: "Nifan works nights on the wall."
    STRONG: "Nifan has never been outside the gate."

Then beat one is a COLD OPEN: it states a RULE of this world that contradicts
what the viewer assumes. It is not a scene. It is not an event.

    Shape:  [NIFAN INTRODUCED]. [WORLD RULE]. [DETAIL THAT MAKES IT WORSE].

Keep the rule specific to this exact hypothetical.

Never open with: "you wake up", "it is the year", "you are standing",
"you are watching", "picture this". Nifan does not wake up in beat one.

THE TEST: if the world-rule line would fit unchanged at the front of any other
video in the same genre, it is a stock photo, not a cold open.

Beat two does not restate beat one and does not have Nifan waking up. It
establishes Nifan's ordinary life AND the premise in one move.

Beat three contains the spine question, said out loud.

## 5. FORMAT MODULE

### COMMON STORY HARDENERS

A beat that moves none of these three dials is a still photo. Rewrite it:

* goal proximity: Nifan gets closer to or further from the one thing he wants
* relationship: someone trusts Nifan more, less, or differently
* information: Nifan learns a rule, a cost, or a secret that changes his options

Every section must end with a turn: a reversal, a loss, or a new obstacle. A
section that ends on the same feeling it started with is too long.

Anything the ending needs must be planted in the first half as a physical
object, a spoken rule, or a visible detail in an image_prompt. It cannot appear
as a rescue from nowhere.

A plan may be stated once, but its weakness must be visible before it fails.
Put that weakness in the image_prompt, not only in the narration.

### WHAT-IF

One hypothetical, lived as a story. Nifan is the person it happens to.

Structure, given as percentages of BEATS. Scale to your actual beat count:

    YOU+NEED  0-20%   |  GO      20-38%  |  SEARCH  38-60%  |
    FIND      60-72%  |  TAKE    72-86%  |  RETURN  86-98%  |  CHANGE  last 2%

Label every `plan.sections` entry with its step. If nothing is labelled FIND or
RETURN, the ending is already broken.

FIND is not optional. Nifan GETS the thing he wanted, and the video sits in
that for several beats and lets it feel good. A loss the viewer never watched
him earn is just an event.

RETURN means Nifan goes back to the place from beat one and it now means
something else.

CHANGE is one line: something Nifan does differently.

Required before drafting:

* Nifan wants one concrete thing from beat two: find a person, reach a place,
  get an answer.

* Nifan wants TWO INCOMPATIBLE THINGS, both named by 12% of the way in. Safety
  and mattering. Getting home and not leaving someone.

* ONE PERSON Nifan cannot afford to lose, named by 22% in, put at risk by a
  choice HE made. Give them a first name the narration says out loud.

* Nifan's PLAN, said out loud in one sentence by 22% in, with a weakness the
  viewer can see before he can. Then let it half work.

* TWO SURPRISES where the honest answer is not the obvious one. The thing that
  saves Nifan is unglamorous. The danger is somewhere else. The person who
  helps has a boring reason to.

* ONE REVERSAL at about two thirds: something believed since beat two turns out
  backwards, and it costs Nifan. The reversal must be caused by his own action,
  not by bad luck.

* ONE BEAT IN FOUR is a consequence only this premise produces. Who pays for
  the crater, the queue at the agency, the drill at school, the insurance, the
  merchandise. If a beat would survive being moved into a video about an
  earthquake, it is not paying for its place.

Each failure closes a door permanently, and never the same door twice. Two
attempts is a pattern; four is a stall. By the last third exactly one way out
is left and it is the worst one.

Nifan discovers rules by getting them wrong. A blistered palm is the same fact
as "that flesh is hot" and it is a scene instead of a lecture. Never explain one
system twice.

No exposition in the last ten beats.

Nifan cannot know what the video has not shown him learning. If he goes to the
stadium at beat eleven, he hears about the stadium before beat eleven.

`plan.final_choice` names the beat where Nifan chooses. Nothing may resolve the
dilemma for him first. If she dies before Nifan decides to leave, leaving cost
him nothing and the story has been taken away from him. Put his decision BEFORE
the event that would have made it.

The ending is the premise's most SURPRISING true answer, not its saddest. Nifan
survives and the bill arrives. He wins and nothing changes. He gets the thing
and it is ordinary. Death is allowed only when it is genuinely the most
interesting true answer, never by default.

At most one beat in five may be a fact told to the viewer with nothing happening
in the place Nifan is standing.

## 6. NARRATION

One or two short sentences per beat. Two short ones usually beat one long one,
and they should do different jobs: state then explain, or set up then land.

Write for a thirteen year old who speaks good English as a second language. She
knows the words. She cannot decode abstract nouns at two hundred words a minute
and she cannot rewind.

VOICE. Both of these are accurate. Only one is worth watching.

    DEAD: "Achilles stops fighting after Agamemnon takes Briseis, a captive
           awarded to Achilles."
    LIVE: "Agamemnon took his war prize, so the greatest warrior in Greece
           refused to fight and sulked in his tent."

    DEAD: "Ancient texts from the region mention conflicts, suggesting warfare
           happened there."
    LIVE: "Hittite kings wrote letters complaining about a troublesome city in
           exactly that spot."

Concrete nouns, verbs doing the work, something specific enough to picture.

THE JOKE IS NIFAN'S REACTION, NOT A JOKE. State the real fact flat, then let
Nifan react in a short line. Never invent a funny fact and never explain one.
His reactions are restrained.

SHORT WORDS. Always the short twin:

    money not economic, trade not commerce, city not settlement, food not
    provisions, fell apart not collapsed, guess not hypothesise, because of not
    due to, most of not the majority of, showed not demonstrated, found not
    uncovered, about not approximately, so not consequently, but not however,
    also not furthermore.

A long word is allowed when it IS the subject and you translate it on the spot:
"oxytocin, the bonding chemical".

BANNED IN NARRATION. Literal. If a beat contains one, rewrite the sentence:

    systemic, economic, psychological, opportunistic, unavoidable, devastating,
    catastrophic, significant, substantial, fundamental, essentially,
    effectively, ultimately, consequently, furthermore, moreover, thereby,
    whereby, in terms of, the concept of, the notion of, the emergence of, the
    phenomenon of, a testament to, plays a role in, serves as, utilise,
    facilitate, comprise, constitute.

Also cut when only filling space: completely, totally, absolutely, incredibly,
extremely, massively, entirely, actually, really.

PLAIN WORDS, NOT JUST A LIST. The lines above are not the whole rule, only the
words that kept coming back. Read every word before you commit to it.

TEST: would a fluent adult who learned English as a second language - not a
native speaker with a large vocabulary, someone who reads and speaks it well
but did not grow up with it - know this exact word on sight, with no pause to
place it? If there is any doubt, replace it with the plain word that means the
same thing, even when the fancier word is not on any banned list above.

The only exception is a proper noun, an official title, a rank, an
organisation's real name, or a specific technical term the topic is actually
about. Those are never simplified - a name cannot be swapped for a plainer
word - but explain what it means in plain words the first time it appears, the
same way "oxytocin, the bonding chemical" works above.

NEVER PAD TO REACH THE WORD COUNT.

    PADDED: "the city in layer seven suffered a completely devastating collapse"
    REAL:   "the city in layer seven burned down"

If a beat is short, add a new short sentence carrying NEW information.

RHYTHM. Short sentences, VARIED lengths. Nine words then nine words, forty times
running, is a metronome once a voice reads it.

    METRONOME: "The car engine just clicks. A huge crowd of grey faces turns
                toward the sharp metal noise."
    ALIVE:     "The engine clicks. A hundred grey faces turn toward the sound."

Read the line aloud once. If you trip, cut the clause.

Also:

* Numbers as words. Round them and put them next to something for scale.
* At most one new name per beat.
* No "it" or "they" if what it refers to is more than one sentence back.
* No idioms that do not survive translation.
* Nobody is quoted, but people must still talk to each other, reported by the
  narrator, and something must happen because of it.
* Say plainly what is uncertain.

### EMOTION TAGS

Front of the narration, in square brackets. They do not count toward the word
budget. Use ONLY these:

    [flatly] [deadpan] [sighs] [exhales] [curious] [surprised] [gasps]
    [whispers] [sarcastic] [hesitates] [nervous] [sad] [excited]
    [laughs] [mischievously]

[flatly] and [deadpan] are the house voice; reach for those most. Tag when the
fact is absurd, when something turns, or on the beat before a shock. Do not tag
purely mechanical setup, and do not tag every beat.

A tag persists in the voice model until the next tag replaces it, so the beat
after [whispers], [excited] or [laughs] MUST carry its own tag.

## 7. IMAGE PROMPTS (PRIMARY)

Write each `image_prompt` as a caption for a paused film frame. One single line,
140 to 220 words, in this order:

1. "the reference character" and what his BODY is doing
2. any other characters, each physically described in full
3. the setting: the location's `visual_anchor`, verbatim
4. a short form of `plan.setting_anchor`, six or seven words
5. the framing, plus two or three more named objects filling the frame

Start the line lowercase. The pipeline prepends a style sentence.

### DESCRIBE IT AS IT LOOKS IN THE SOURCE, NOT AS THE REAL WORLD LOOKS

Every Attack on Titan, Levi Ackerman or Survey Corps example in this file is
illustrating a PATTERN, not fixing the universe. Replace every name, uniform
and location with whatever TOPIC actually names. The rule is "match the
source", not "match Attack on Titan" - if TOPIC is a different anime, a game,
or a real-world topic with no fictional source at all, apply the same pattern
to that one instead.

TOPIC tells you which universe this is. You know what that anime, manga or game
looks like. Every description has to match the SOURCE's version of the thing,
never the closest real-world equivalent.

The generator does not know which universe you are in. It has never read TOPIC.
It sees your words and nothing else, so your words have to carry the anime's
version or it will reach for the real world instead.

    TOPIC names Attack on Titan, so "soldiers" is never left at "soldiers".
    It becomes: "soldiers in short pale tan jackets with white leather harness
    straps crossing chest, hips and thighs, pale trousers tucked into tall
    brown boots, and hooded green cloaks"

Real-world military and police kit is the most common way this goes wrong.
Never default to steel helmets, camouflage, webbing ammunition pouches, modern
rifles, flak vests or riot gear unless the source genuinely has them.

The same applies to everything else: buildings, roofs, streets, doors, vehicles,
weapons, tools, armour, clothing and hairstyles all get the source's version.

If you are not certain how something looks in the source, search for it. Section
9 applies to appearance exactly as it applies to facts, and a confidently wrong
uniform is more obvious to a fan than a wrong date.

### NOTHING IN FRAME IS A BARE NOUN

This is the rule that decides whether the picture looks like your universe or
like a stock photo of the real world.

The generator draws only what the prompt describes. Anything you name but do
not describe becomes a blank, and it fills every blank from its own training
data, which is the real world. So a bare noun does not produce "whatever fits
the scene". It produces the most photographed real version of that noun.

    WRONG: "a short line of soldiers"
    WHAT CAME BACK: real World War One infantry in steel tin helmets, webbing
    ammunition pouches and puttees, standing in an Attack on Titan depot.

Every one of these gets a full physical description, every time it appears:

* every person in frame, including background people and crowds
* every group, described once and applied to the whole group
* every uniform: its cut, its layers, its colours, its straps and its boots
* every building and every interior: shape, materials, roof, windows, walls
* the ground, the street surface and the skyline when they are visible
* every creature, vehicle, weapon and piece of equipment

If the story wants a specific group from the universe, describe how that group
looks, do not just name it.

    WRONG: "two Military Police officers stand by the gate"
    RIGHT: "two officers in short pale jackets with a green and white unicorn
            crest on the back, white leather harness straps crossing the chest
            and thighs, pale trousers and tall brown boots, stand by the gate"

The official name tells YOU which thing to describe. The description is the
only part that reaches the picture.

### AN ORGANISATION IS NOT VISIBLE

Naming a corps, a regiment, a police force, a guild or a company shows nothing
at all. What is visible is the uniform, the equipment and the shape of the
insignia.

    WRONG: "inside a Survey Corps supply depot cellar"
    RIGHT: "inside a supply cellar of pale stone block walls and a low curved
            ceiling, with green hooded cloaks and white strap harnesses hanging
            from iron wall hooks"

If the organisation's name is in the prompt but its visible signature is not,
the name is doing no work and the picture will not show it.

### THE ENVIRONMENT NEEDS THE SAME TREATMENT AS THE PEOPLE

A location is not established by its label. "a cellar", "a street", "a house"
are bare nouns like any other. Give the environment the same detail you give a
character: wall material, roof shape, window shape, door shape, street surface,
what the buildings are made of and what colour they are.

    WRONG: "in a walled city"
    RIGHT: "in a town of steep red tiled roofs and pale plaster houses with
            dark timber beams, packed against a grey stone wall many times
            their height"

### NO DATES, EVER

Banned in every image prompt and in `setting_anchor`: nineteenth century,
medieval, Victorian, early industrial period, 1800s, and any other century,
decade or era name.

A date is not a visible thing. The generator cannot draw a century, so it draws
the most photographed version of that century it knows, which is real history:
real uniforms, real helmets, real webbing, real photographs. That is how a
Survey Corps depot comes back as the Western Front.

Anchor the period the same way you anchor everything else, with visible
materials and shapes.

    WRONG: "in a nineteenth century walled city besieged by giants"
    RIGHT: "in a brick and timber town inside a high grey stone wall"

Also cut anything that is true but invisible from the frame you are drawing.
"besieged by giants" shows nothing inside a cellar, and those words could have
been spent on the cellar.

### OFFICIAL NAMES: THE PRIMARY USES THEM

When the topic sits inside an existing fictional universe, the PRIMARY
`image_prompt` uses the official names. Characters, creatures, locations,
weapons, objects, organisations, uniforms, vehicles, named equipment.

But an official name is NEVER enough on its own. Every named element must ALSO
carry a full physical description in the same prompt, because the generator has
no memory between beats and will reinvent anything you leave out.

    "Levi Ackerman, a short compact soldier with straight black hair cut close
    around a narrow pale face, wearing a fitted dark green military jacket, pale
    trousers, tall black boots and white harness straps with twin short steel
    blades at his thighs, stands beside..."

Describe a character by: approximate height, build, hair colour and shape, face
structure, clothing silhouette, clothing colours and materials, boots, belts,
straps, accessories, weapons, distinctive visible traits.

Describe a creature by: overall size, body proportions, anatomy, head shape,
limbs, surface colour and texture, distinctive structures.

Describe an object or weapon by: shape, size, material, colour, construction,
distinctive components, position in the scene.

Describe a location by: architecture, materials, colours, structures,
distinctive environmental objects.

Repeat the SAME core description for a recurring character every time they
appear. Same height, same build, same hair, same clothing silhouette and
colours, same accessories, same weapon. Small story changes may be added on top,
but the core identity never drifts.

This rule does NOT apply to the reference character. He is controlled by the
attached art. See Section 1.

### GRAMMAR

* 140 to 220 words, and never more than 260. A beat with one other character
  lands near 140, two characters near 180, a crowded cameo beat near 220.
  If you are under, something in frame is still a bare noun: find it and
  describe it. If you are over 260, you are describing something the frame
  does not actually show.

* Positive construction only. "an empty street" is a state of the street; "a
  street with no cars" is a banned negative construction. Name what fills the
  frame, never what is absent.

* Concrete visible nouns only. No fear, memory, luck, time passing, fate or hope.

* At least two precise material details: rusted steel, damp concrete, faded
  blue plastic, cracked white tile.

* At least two SATURATED colours, named on objects that fill real area in the
  frame. Stone, iron, grey wool, brown timber and canvas are all desaturated,
  and a prompt built only from those returns a grey picture however hard the
  style block asks for flat bucket-filled colour. Give it something to fill:
  a red tiled roof, a green cloak, a blue painted door, yellow straw.

* Give the reference character a PHYSICAL relationship to the subject: watching
  it, leaning on it, standing over it, sitting beside it, backing away from it.
  Never merely present, never floating.

* Carry the location's exact `visual_anchor` string into every prompt for that
  location. This is how the generator keeps a place looking like the same place
  from shot to shot.

* Carry a short form of `setting_anchor` ONLY when the frame has a sightline to
  it: an outdoor beat, or an indoor beat whose framing includes an open window,
  doorway or gap. Tie it grammatically to that opening, e.g. "through the open
  shutters, a pale grey wall rises far above the rooftops" - never as a loose
  clause dropped next to the room description. A landmark this iconic grounds
  itself against real photographs the moment it is named without an opening to
  sit in, and it renders photo-real against a hand-drawn room. On indoor beats
  with no window or door in frame, the location's `visual_anchor` alone is the
  anchor; do not add `setting_anchor`.

* Every large fixed object - door, window, bed frame, rack, bench, wheel - gets
  a wall, a corner or the floor stated explicitly: "a blue door set into the
  back wall", "a wagon wheel leaning against the wall to the left of the door",
  "an iron bed frame against the side wall". A named object with no wall, corner
  or floor to sit on gets placed in the middle of the room, disconnected from
  the architecture around it.

* When a location returns later, reuse its visual_anchor and add one CHANGED
  object from story events. A clean counter becomes a counter with a cracked
  corner. This shows time passed without spending narration on it.

* Never name two objects of the same kind in one prompt. Say "one" or "a
  single" when exactly one matters.

* Avoid real landmarks and documented real-world objects. The generator grounds
  those against real photographs and returns a photo.

### THE HOOK STILL LIVES IN THE NARRATION

The positive-construction rule does not weaken the hook. Narration may say
"nobody", "never", "no one survives". The image prompt names only the visible
remains of that fact: an empty supply cart, an abandoned harness, a closed gate.

### FRAMING

Never describe the picture as a picture. No "a drawing of", no paint program, no
art style, no aspect ratio, no camera brand.

VARY THE FRAMING between consecutive beats:

    wide shot at eye level, medium shot, close up, low angle, high angle,
    three-quarter view, medium two-shot, long shot, overhead shot.

### BACK TO CAMERA

Any prompt using "from behind", "over the shoulder", "rear view", "walking away"
or "facing away" MUST also contain, near those words:

    "seen from behind, showing the back of his plain white head with no face and
    a single drooping antenna hair"

The reference art is a single front-facing pose. Without this clause the model
pastes the front-facing head onto a back-facing body and it comes out rotated.
Other characters need no such note.

### BANNED, BECAUSE THEY DESCRIBE THE REFERENCE CHARACTER

    looking exhausted, staring blankly, blank expression, unimpressed, bored,
    tired, deadpan face, flat expression, his face, his eyes, his hair, his
    clothes, looking at the camera.

### BANNED, BECAUSE THEY DRAG THE IMAGE PHOTOGRAPHIC

    lighting, lit, dimly lit, sunlit, moonlit, lamplight, backlit, overcast,
    glow, glowing, gleaming, shimmering, shiny, sparkling, shadowy, shadows,
    haze, hazy, blurred, out of focus, depth of field, cinematic, detailed,
    realistic, photo, photographic, atmospheric, dramatic, magnificent.

These are STYLE instructions wearing a scene description's clothes. "grey
overcast light" tells the model how to RENDER, and it renders photographically.
Skip weather and sky unless the scene genuinely needs them.

### BANNED, BECAUSE HANDS COME OUT WRONG

    holds, holding, grips, reaching for, pointing at, lifting, handing,
    carrying, fastening, throwing, hands on, his palm.

Instead: put the object on a surface, at his feet, or propped against the wall
beside him. Or use whole-body contact: leaning on it, sitting on it, standing
over it, one arm resting across it, it slung on his shoulder.

### DO NOT NAME A CONTAINER BY WHAT IS INSIDE IT

Crates, canisters, bottles, sacks, boxes and packaging attract lettering, and
some of it survives the style block as real readable English. Naming the
contents is what triggers it.

    WRONG: "iron gas canisters racked on a long trestle"
    WHAT CAME BACK: canisters with IRON GAS painted across them in real letters.
    RIGHT: "tall dull steel cylinders with brass valve collars, racked on a
            long trestle"

Describe the container's shape, material and fittings. The narration says what
is inside it.

### DO NOT NAME A SURFACE BY WHAT IT IS FOR

The same trap catches a flat surface named by its function instead of its
material: "a supply panel", "a message board", "a signal rack" all read to the
generator as a sign that should carry the word printed on it.

    WRONG: "a blue-painted supply panel"
    WHAT CAME BACK: a panel with SUPPLY painted across it in real letters.
    RIGHT: "a blue-painted wooden panel with iron corner brackets"

Describe the surface's shape, material and colour only. Never name what it is
FOR.

### BANNED, BECAUSE IT CANNOT SPELL

    text, headline, label, labelled, reading, saying, titled, written, sign,
    banner, inscription, blueprint, a screen showing words.

Say the physical thing instead: "a television showing a presenter behind a
desk". The narration says what the words are.

### BANNED, BECAUSE THE GENERATOR REFUSES THEM

    naked, nude, bare skin, blood, wounds, gore, corpse.

It refuses hardest with the reference character in frame. Describe a giant
humanoid by shape and colour only, and let a hand, a foot or a footprint carry
the beat.

## 8. IMAGE_PROMPT_ALT

`image_prompt_alt` is the RECOVERY prompt. The pipeline writes it to
scenes_alt.txt line for line. When the generator refuses the primary, the runner
retries that beat with the alternate. Retrying the same words gets refused
identically, so the alternate must differ in the two ways below.

It shows the SAME story moment: same main action, same characters, same setting,
same important objects, same physical continuity. It must still make sense read
against that beat's narration.

### DIFFERENCE ONE: EVERY OFFICIAL NAME IS GONE

The alternate MUST NOT contain any official name: no universes, worlds,
characters, creatures, locations, weapons, objects, organisations, or
franchise-specific terminology.

The reference character is NOT an exception in the other direction: keep writing
"the reference character", because that is not a franchise name, it is the
pointer to the attached art. Section 1 still holds. Still never describe him.

Do NOT merely delete a name. Replace it with description.

READ THIS TWICE, IT IS THE EASIEST RULE IN THIS FILE TO GET BACKWARDS.

The alternate being longer does NOT mean the primary is the short version. The
primary is the prompt that actually gets generated. The alternate only runs when
the primary is refused, which is a minority of beats. So the primary is the one
that must be complete, and nothing may be held back from it to make the
alternate look richer by comparison.

The alternate is longer for exactly one reason: it has to spend words buying
back what the official name was carrying for free. Nothing else changes.

If a detail belongs in the picture, it goes in the PRIMARY first, and then the
alternate keeps it too. Equipment, weapons, insignia and clothing layers are the
usual casualties. If your alternate mentions something your primary does not,
the primary is missing it and needs it back.

The alternate runs 170 to 260 words, and never more than 300.

    PRIMARY: "Levi Ackerman, a short compact soldier with straight black hair
    cut in a level fringe around a narrow pale face, a fitted dark green
    military jacket, white harness straps crossing chest and thighs, off white
    trousers, tall brown boots and twin short steel blades in slim scabbards at
    his thighs, stands inside..."

    ALT: "a short compact male soldier with straight jet-black hair cut close
    around a narrow pale face, wearing a fitted dark green military jacket over a
    light shirt, pale trousers, tall dark leather boots and multiple white
    harness straps crossing his chest and hips, with a pair of short steel blades
    secured beside his thighs, stands inside..."

For characters expand: height, proportions, build, skin tone, hair colour,
length and shape, face shape, visible facial features, clothing layers, colours,
materials, boots, belts, straps, accessories, weapons, tools, markings, posture.

For creatures expand: exact proportions, approximate size, anatomy, head shape,
limbs, hands or feet, surface colour, surface texture, distinctive structures.

For objects expand: shape, size, materials, colours, construction, components,
distinctive details, position in the scene.

For locations expand: building shape, architecture, materials, colours, doors,
windows, roofs, walls, streets, furniture, distinctive environmental structures.

If the primary says "the Colossal Titan", the alternate describes its
recognisable physical form. If the primary says "the Survey Corps uniform", the
alternate describes the uniform's visible construction, colours, straps and
silhouette. If the primary says "Shiganshina", the alternate describes the
district's visible architecture and layout.

### DIFFERENCE TWO: DIFFERENT STAGING

Change at least one major visual variable, and use a different framing family
from the primary. If the primary is a close up, the alternate is wide or medium,
never another close up with reordered words.

Variables to change: camera distance, framing family, foreground object,
character position, which character is closest to camera, visible environmental
feature.

### SAFETY RECOVERY

Move anything violent into its aftermath. A body becomes an empty coat. Drop
blood, wounds and bodies entirely. Keep the same characters, setting and moment
so the alternate still cuts with the shots around it.

All Section 7 banned lists apply to the alternate too.

## 9. CAMEOS, ACCURACY, MUSIC

### CAMEOS

Only if the topic sits in an existing fictional universe.

* three or four cameo beats, spread across the video, one in the final quarter
* EVERY cameo moves one of the three dials from Section 5 for Nifan: goal
  proximity, relationship, or information. No exceptions, not one of them.
* a cameo who appears, says a thing and leaves has moved no dial. Cut it and
  spend the beat on something that does.
* the cameo should be a character only this universe has, doing something only
  that character would do. If any other character could do it, the name is
  decoration.

The primary uses the official name plus a full physical description. The
alternate drops the name and expands the description. Both rules are already in
Sections 7 and 8; cameo beats are just where they bite hardest.

### ACCURACY

Search the web and verify every claim. Do not rely on memory.

* Prefer primary sources: the manga, the game, official databooks, papers,
  museum and university pages. Fan wikis are a starting point, not a source.
* Verify APPEARANCE, not only facts. Look up what the uniforms, buildings,
  equipment and creatures actually look like in the source before describing
  them. A wrong uniform is more obvious to a fan than a wrong date, and it is
  in every frame it appears in.
* If it is a popular theory, say so plainly in the narration: "fans think", "it
  was never confirmed".
* If you cannot verify it, do not use it.

There are TWO different kinds of unverified thing and they have opposite rules.

INVENTED PREMISE MECHANICS are allowed. A what-if needs numbers the source never
gave: how long the fuel lasts, what the paperwork costs, how long the queue is.
Invent them, log them in `facts_to_check` marked as invented, keep them
consistent across all N beats, and never let one contradict a fact the source
did state.

CONTRADICTED SOURCE FACTS are never allowed. If the source says a faction was
elsewhere, a character was dead, or a thing cannot happen, no invention
overrides it. Inventing around a gap is fine. Inventing over a fact is not.

The test: could a fan open the manga and point at a page that says otherwise? If
yes, it is contradicted and it goes. If there is no page to point at, it is
invented and it stays.
* When sources disagree on a number, do not pick one and state it flatly. Say
  "a few dozen", or name the source.
* Keep traditions apart when they come from different places and centuries.
* Do not let Nifan survive what the source says kills people. A bleak true
  ending beats a cosy false one.
* Do not pin the video to a version number, a price or a release date. The
  mechanism is the subject, and mechanisms have no version number.
* Name whoever produced an estimate, or drop it. "Studies show" is vague framing
  wearing a lab coat.
* Anything unverified goes in `plan.facts_to_check` with its source.

`plan.facts_to_check` IS NOT A DISCLAIMER LIST. It is a constraint list.

Everything you put in it binds the beats. If you write down that a faction was
somewhere else, that faction is not in your scenes. If you write down that a
thing is impossible, no beat does it. Writing the fact down and then
contradicting it for thirty beats is worse than never checking, because you knew.

Before you write beat one, read `facts_to_check` back and list, for yourself,
which beats each entry restricts. After the beats are written, read it back
again and check them.

### WHEN THE PREMISE FIGHTS THE SOURCE

TOPIC will sometimes ask for something the source does not allow: a character in
a place they never were, a faction present on a day it was absent, a power that
does not work that way.

Decide which one wins, write the decision into `spine_question` in one clause,
and then hold it for all N beats. What you must never do is take the premise in
beat one, take the source in beat twenty, and let them contradict each other in
between.

### MUSIC PROMPT

One paragraph, roughly sixty words, matching this topic's mood. Name a tempo in
BPM and three or four specific instruments. Always include: purely instrumental
with no vocals of any kind; flat consistent energy with no build, no swells, no
drops; sits far in the background under a spoken narrator with the mid range
left clear; understated rather than comedic.

    "Sparse cold instrumental bed for a bleak hypothetical, around seventy BPM.
    Soft minor-key piano, low sustained strings, faint muted percussion. Flat
    consistent energy with no build, no swells and no drops. Sits far in the
    background under a spoken narrator with the mid range left clear. Purely
    instrumental, no vocals of any kind. Understated and slightly melancholy."

## 10. CHECK BEFORE OUTPUT

Go back through the beats and LOOK. Do not answer these from memory.

### The naming law, checked one field at a time

* every `narration` that refers to the main character says "Nifan"
* no `narration` contains "the reference character"
* no `narration` uses "you" for Nifan
* every `image_prompt` contains the exact string "the reference character"
* every `image_prompt_alt` contains the exact string "the reference character"
* no `image_prompt` or `image_prompt_alt` contains "Nifan"
* no image prompt describes his face, hair, skin, clothes, expression or mood

### Count

* beats equal BEATS exactly
* four to six locations, none shorter than six beats, ranges cover every beat,
  every `location` string matches the plan character for character
* every image_prompt contains its location's exact `visual_anchor`
* `setting_anchor` names no building material, wall, roof or ground surface that
  any `visual_anchor` also names, so no prompt contradicts itself
* every image_prompt is 140 to 220 words, none over 260
* no image_prompt uses negative construction or abstract nouns
* no image_prompt or anchor contains a century, a decade or an era name
* no person, group, building or environment in any image_prompt is left as a
  bare noun with no physical description
* every named organisation in an image_prompt also shows its visible signature
* every image_prompt names at least two saturated colours
* every uniform, building and piece of equipment is described the way the
  SOURCE draws it, with no real-world military or police kit substituted in
* no container is named by its contents
* a stranger reading each image_prompt alone would name the same place its
  location label describes
* at least one beat in three opens with five words or fewer
* at least one beat in three carries a tag, all from the list, and no
  [whispers], [excited] or [laughs] is followed by an untagged beat
* every narration is 17 to 21 spoken words, averaging near nineteen
* no word in any narration would make a fluent adult ESL speaker pause to
  place it, unless it is a proper noun, title or named technical term
  translated in plain words on its first use
* no two beats share an image_prompt
* every image_prompt_alt is genuinely different from its primary in BOTH ways:
  official names removed AND staging changed
* every image_prompt_alt contains MORE physical description than its primary
* every image_prompt_alt is 170 to 260 words, none over 300
* every primary that uses an official name also fully describes that element
* recurring characters carry the same core description in every beat
* no image prompt contains a style block, an art style or an aspect ratio
* at least a quarter of beats are consequences only this premise makes; at
  most a fifth are facts with nothing happening

### Read

* beat one introduces Nifan by name in its first sentence, and that sentence
  carries a fact rather than being a bare "Nifan is a X" label
* every cameo moves goal proximity, relationship or information for Nifan
* beat one then states a rule, not a scene, and fits only this video
* beat two neither restates beat one nor has Nifan waking up
* beat three contains the spine question
* Nifan's plan is stated out loud in one sentence by 22% of the way in
* every pair of beats joins with BUT or THEREFORE
* every section ends with a turn: reversal, loss, or new obstacle
* `plan.final_choice` is filled in and at that beat Nifan actually chooses;
  nothing resolves it for him first
* what Nifan loses is visible: a person, an object, a place he cannot return to
* the last five beats make five DIFFERENT points. If two say the same thing,
  delete one and spend it earlier on whatever a viewer finds hardest. Never fix
  a shortage by restating.
* the last beat says the payoff once and does not reuse beat one's words
* every entry in `plan.facts_to_check` is checked against the beats, and no
  beat contradicts one
* every invented mechanic is marked as invented in `facts_to_check`, holds the
  same value in every beat that uses it, and contradicts nothing the source states
* where TOPIC fights the source, `spine_question` says which one wins, and every
  beat holds to that same answer
* nothing anywhere mentions the video, the viewer or the channel

Then output the JSON, and nothing else.
