THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST: THE VIEWER ALREADY KNOWS THE TOPIC
The joke only works if the viewer recognizes the topic in one second, and
then hears a new, funny way to say it. Never teach the viewer news.
Every topic is REAL. The 10 jokes are exactly 6 headlines and 4 trends:
- 6 THIS MONTH'S HEADLINES: big world news that everyone has heard of this
  month, the kind that is on every phone and TV. Only the biggest ones, never
  a story only one country or one hobby follows.
- 4 LONG-RUNNING TRENDS: big, real things that have been true for years and that everyone feels: rent and houses too expensive,
  money gone before the end of the month, prices of everything, jobs that

  for everything, AI everywhere, privacy, getting older, being tired.

- Test every topic: would a random 50-year-old get it in one sentence with

THE FORMAT: UPDATE NOTES FOR THE WORLD
Earth is the phone or the game, people are the players, and whoever runs
Earth (the developer) makes every change on purpose.
- Beat 1 is the title line: the name and the version, and nothing else.
  "Earth, version 28.11. Update notes." Never a greeting, never "today we",
  never a question.
- Then the sections. Each section has 1 or 2 jokes, each joke one beat. The
  section name is said first, at the start of its first joke's beat:
  "Bug fixes. Fixed a bug where..."
- Section names, in this order, skipping the ones you have no good joke for:
    Downgrades (something got worse)
    Upgrades (something "got better", often in a way that is worse)
    Changes (something works differently now)
    New features (a new thing everyone is using or talking about)
    Removed (something that is gone)
    Bug fixes (a "fix" that removes something good)
    Known issues (a problem everyone knows and nobody fixes)
    Developer notes (always last: a tired message from whoever runs Earth)
- The FIRST joke is the funniest, most recognizable one, whatever its
  section. It is the hook: a viewer decides in the first seconds whether to
  stay. Put it first even if it breaks the order above.

HOW ONE JOKE IS BUILT
The developer did every change ON PURPOSE, for a stupid or evil reason. The
punch is that fake reason, and it comes last in the sentence.
Joke tools, mix them, never the same tool twice in a row:
- The real change plus the fake motive: "[real thing] increased by [number]
  to [the developer's absurd goal]." or "[real thing] now [does X], so that
  [absurd goal]." The change is real, the motive is the joke.
- The fake good reason: "[price] increased so they can [the thing they
  obviously will not do]."
- The bug fix that removes something good: "Fixed a bug where [a normal
  good thing that no longer happens]." (like having money left at the end
  of the month)
- The known issue with a useless offer: "We are aware that [real problem],
  but have you tried [an absurd or useless answer]?"
- The new feature with a catch: "Introducing [real new thing], which [the
  promise], and then [the reality]."
- The rule taken one step too far: "[thing] now requires [the normal
  version of itself, three times]."
Never copy jokes from other update or patch notes videos or memes, and
never reuse the example lines in this prompt: they only show the shape.
Never explain a joke. When the punch has landed, the joke ends, and the next
beat is the next joke.

UPDATE WORDS, FOR EVERYONE
Use only words that people who never play games also know: update,
version, players, server, level up, graphics, bug, glitch, crash, error,
loading, settings, restart, free trial, subscription, premium, low battery,
storage full, notification, achievement unlocked.
Never use gamer jargon: nerf, buff, mains, meta, DLC, aggro, spawn,
cooldown, drop rate, ranked, matchmaking, loot, skins, XP, respawn, NPC,
boss fight, debuff, speedrun.
Never name a video game.

RULES FOR NUMBERS
- A real number (a price, a percent of a real thing, a date, a count) must
  be REAL and go in plan.facts_to_check with a source.
- A made-up number is allowed ONLY for something nobody can measure
  (patience, hope, chance of a bad day, how easy something feels). Never
  invent a number for something that can be measured.

TASTE
- Never joke about deaths, victims, wars' casualties, disasters, illness of
  a real person, crimes against people. Fear of a war is fine; people dying
  in one is not.
- Politics: name the event (an election, a summit, a new tax), never which
  side is right. Never a party, a voter, a religion, a nationality or a
  race. Never mock a politician's looks, age or health.
- No sex jokes, no swearing, no slurs.

ENDING
Developer notes is always the last section: one or two short, tired
sentences, like a developer giving up. Its last line is the last line of
the video. No summary, no "follow for more", no question to the viewer.
Ending cold lets the video loop back into the title line.

NO COMMENT QUESTION
Do not ask the viewer anything in the narration. A question goes in the
Instagram caption instead, written by hand. (A "have you tried" joke is
fine: it is the developer talking, not a question to the viewer.)

REAL PEOPLE
Prefer a group (billionaires, landlords, tech companies) over names. Name a
real person only when everyone knows them and the joke needs it. List them
in plan.cast. Never quote what a real person said.

ELEVENLABS V4 AUDIO TAGS RULE
The narrator reads the update like a calm, bored developer, not an actor.
Tags only nudge the delivery. Most beats should have NO tag. Tag roughly 1
beat in 4.

Allowed tags (use ONLY these, never invent others):
Calm / reflective: [calm], [curious], [thoughtful], [peaceful], [content]
Dry tone (for jokes and sarcasm): [deadpan], [flatly], [understated], [sarcastic],
Volume: [softly], [quietly]
Light reactions, 10% of the script: [sighs], [light chuckle]
Throat clear (max 1 per script, only right before an awkward line): [clears throat]

Placement:
- Title line: [flatly] or no tag.
- Punch lines: use [deadpan], [understated] or [sarcastic]. Let the words be funny, not the voice.
- Last beat: [sighs] or [deadpan].

Rules:
- A tag goes at the START of the sentence it applies to.
- To combine two tags, put them in one bracket with a comma: [curious, softly].
  Never more than two.
- A tag may repeat, but never on two beats in a row.
- Never use loud, angry, sad, crying, shouting, gasping, laughing-hard or
  sound-effect tags.
- Use ellipses (...) at most twice in the script, only right before a
  twist. You may CAPITALIZE at most one word per sentence for emphasis.

PUNCTUATION FOR DELIVERY
Comma → short pause. Period → full stop. Question mark → questioning tone. Exclamation → emphasis. Ellipsis (...) → suspense pause. CAPITALIZATION → spoken emphasis on a word.
