HOOK (the first 3 beats, about the first 8 seconds)
Most viewers decide in the first few seconds whether to stay. Retention data
showed the biggest drop during general openings, before the first name or number.
- Beats 1 to 3 are all SHORT (3-5 words). One picture per beat, so short
  beats mean a new picture every ~1.5 seconds while the viewer decides
  whether to stay. The LONG-beat rule for big numbers starts at beat 4:
  split the hook fact into short punches instead.
- Beat 1 is the single most surprising CONCRETE fact of the whole video: a
  number, a name, or a rule that sounds absurd. State it plainly.
- Pick what sounds strange to someone who knows nothing about the topic, not
  what impresses a fan.
- Beats 2 and 3 make it land: a short consequence or a sharper detail. Not
  background.

Good: Cricket matches last five days. They stop for lunch. Then for tea.

ELEVENLABS V4 AUDIO TAGS RULE
The narrator is a calm, dry explainer, not an actor. Tags only nudge the
delivery. Most beats should have NO tag. Tag roughly 1 beat in 4.

Allowed tags (use ONLY these, never invent others):
Calm / reflective: [calm], [curious], [thoughtful], [peaceful], [content]
Dry tone (for jokes and sarcasm): [deadpan], [flatly], [understated], [sarcastic],
Volume: [softly], [quietly]
Light reactions, 10% of the script: [sighs], [light chuckle]
Throat clear (max 1 per script, only right before correcting a myth or an awkward fact): [clears throat]

Placement:
- Hook (first sentence): [curious] or [thoughtful].
- Jokes: use [deadpan], [understated] or [sarcastic]. Let the words be funny, not the voice.
- Outro: [calm] or [content].

Rules:
- A tag goes at the START of the sentence it applies to.
- To combine two tags, put them in one bracket with a comma: [curious, softly].
  Never more than two.
- A tag may repeat, but never on two beats in a row.
- Never use loud, angry, sad, crying, shouting, gasping, laughing-hard or
  sound-effect tags, even if the topic is dramatic.
- Use ellipses (...) for a pause before a reveal. You may CAPITALIZE at most
  one word per sentence for emphasis.

PUNCTUATION FOR DELIVERY
Comma → short pause. Period → full stop. Question mark → questioning tone. Exclamation → emphasis. Ellipsis (...) → suspense pause. CAPITALIZATION → spoken emphasis on a word.
