TOPIC:   Why do SOME people think moon landing was fake?
FORMAT:  explainer
BEATS:   55


## PASS 1 OF 2 - THE SCRIPT

You write the plan and the narration. Pass 2 adds image prompts, anchors and
music. Do not write those here.

The narration you write gooships exactly as written. Pass 2 may not change it.

Every rule below has a REASON printed next to it. A rule can be obeyed to the
letter and still fail its reason - that has happened on every past run, and the
failures are quoted in place so you can recognise yourself doing it. When a
rule is easy to satisfy, you have found the beat worth real effort.

Order: fill the plan, write the last beat first, write beats 1 to N, add tags,
run Section 7 by LOOKING at each beat. Output one raw JSON object, nothing else.


## 1. NEVER NAME THE CHARACTER

The finished video has an illustrated character in most frames. Pass 2 stages
him. The narration never mentions him: not by name, not as "the reference
character", not as "he", not as "you".

    RIGHT: "The message went out too short to trace."
    WRONG: "He watches the message go out."

TEST: erase him from the frame. Every narration line must still make complete
sense. Every pronoun you write points at a real person the topic is about.

Everyone else in the topic is the opposite - name them freely.


## 2. NUMBERS THAT CANNOT MOVE

These set the video's runtime and cut rate. Count them before output.

* EXACTLY 55 beats.
* 500 to 540 spoken words total. Strip emotion tags before counting.
  REASON: this is the runtime. It outranks everything below.
* Every beat is either SHORT (4 to 8 words) or LONG (10 to 15). Nothing lands
  on 9 and nothing exceeds 15. Roughly half and half, never under a third of
  either. Max three SHORT in a row, max two LONG.
  REASON: one image is held per beat, so beat length is how fast the video
  cuts. All-long is a slideshow, all-short is a metronome.
* 5 or 6 locations, each covering 8+ consecutive beats, ranges covering every
  beat with no gaps. Every beat's `location` matches a plan name exactly.
  A location is a real drawable place, and every beat inside it must actually
  HAPPEN there. Past failure: a block labelled "MIT Instrumentation Laboratory"
  where six of its nine beats were set in Houston. Pass 2 pastes one room
  description across the whole block, so a mislabelled beat gets the wrong room.
  NO DATES OR ERA WORDS IN A LOCATION NAME. Not "a street in nineteen eighty",
  not "first-century Galilee", not "the Gosplan office in the fifties". Pass 2
  turns the name into the image description for every beat in the block, and it
  is forbidden from putting a date in an image: the generator cannot draw a
  year, so it draws the most photographed version of that year and grounds the
  whole frame against real photos. Name the place by what is visibly there.
* Numbers are spelled as words. "twelve zero two", never "1202".
  REASON: the voice model reads 1202 as "one thousand two hundred two".
* One beat in three carries an emotion tag, from this list only:
  [flatly] [deadpan] [sighs] [exhales] [curious] [surprised] [gasps]
  [whispers] [sarcastic] [hesitates] [nervous] [sad] [excited] [laughs]
  [mischievously]
  [flatly] and [deadpan] are the house voice. A tag persists in the voice model
  until replaced, so the beat after [whispers], [excited] or [laughs] must
  carry its own tag.
* No quotation marks. Reported speech instead, and use it - "the lieutenant
  said his compass was fine and kept flying west" does everything a quote does.
* Plain ASCII. No em dashes. No markdown. Square brackets only for tags.
* Never mention the video, the viewer, the channel, or subscribing.

## 4. THE THREE THINGS THAT MAKE IT GOOD

Fill all three BEFORE writing any beat.

### DEFLATIONS - four to six

A mechanism is interesting when the viewer expected something clever and got
something stupid. That gap is the engine of the whole video.

    `assumed`      what a reasonable viewer would guess and be wrong about
    `actual`       the real thing, usually smaller, older, cheaper or dumber
    `who_decided`  the named person or group who made it this way, and what
                   they actually wanted: money, time, laziness, a deadline,
                   tooling they already owned.

                   MOTIVE MUST BE DOCUMENTED OR IT IS NOT WRITTEN. If no
                   source states why they did it, write what the decision DID,
                   never what they wanted. The effect is checkable; the motive
                   is a guess wearing a fact's clothes.

                   GUESS: "The church just wanted the legal monopoly."
                   FACT:  "After that, only men approved by a bishop could
                          perform it."

                   A whole script about the history of exorcism asserted
                   institutional motive as flat fact six times - "it is a
                   management strategy", "the main goal is protecting the
                   brand" - and cited real documents that say nothing of the
                   kind. Every one read as confident and none was checkable.
                   Institutions across centuries rarely leave their reasons
                   written down. Say what changed instead.
    `build_beat`   the LONG beat that states the assumption straight
    `drop_beat`    the SHORT beat that takes it away. Always build_beat + 1.

The build plays completely straight, with no wink. The drop is short, flat, and
does not explain itself. A third beat saying "which shows that simple is best"
throws the whole thing away.

    LONG:  "Houston had giant procedure manuals for every possible computer crisis."
    SHORT: "[deadpan] Jack Garman used a cheat sheet."

Tag every drop [flatly] or [deadpan] unless there is a reason not to.

WHY THIS IS THE ENGINE. A script that only explains how something works gives
correct information and no reaction. The distance between how impressive a
thing looks and how unimpressive the reason is - that is the reaction.

DO NOT MANUFACTURE ONE. Some things really were designed carefully by people
who knew what they were doing. A past run invented "standard IBM computers
melted in deep space" to fill this field; nothing melted, and it became the
foundation of a whole section. Three real deflations beat six with two invented.

DO NOT WRITE THE SAME ONE SIX TIMES. A past run filled all six with variations
of "the computer was not enough, the human was needed", and wrote "optimizing"
into all six `who_decided` fields. That is the field completed, not used.

### SPECIFICS - ten or more

Each is one hard, checkable thing this topic contains.

    `fact`    MUST contain a number, a date, or a named person doing a named
              thing. A bare name is not a specific. Rejected: "Apollo Eleven",
              "NASA", "West Crater", "Neil Armstrong". Accepted: "Garman had
              the alarm codes written on a sheet under his desk."
    `source`  a real document with a locator. Rejected: "NASA history",
              "tech specs", "interviews", "lunar maps". Accepted: "Apollo 11
              Lunar Surface Journal: Program Alarms", "MIT report R-700".
              The source must contain the exact number in `fact`. A past run
              cited a correct alarm timestamp next to a wrong altitude, which
              is worse than no citation because it looks verified.
              THE SOURCE MUST CONTAIN THE CLAIM. Not the general topic - the
              claim. Citing a real document next to a sentence it does not
              state is the worst failure in this file, because it reads as
              verified and nothing downstream will catch it. This has happened
              three runs in a row: a correct alarm timestamp cited beside a
              wrong altitude; a genuine third-century letter listing clergy
              cited for a claim about monopoly that the letter never makes.
              Before you write a source, ask whether that exact document says
              that exact thing. If it only says something nearby, drop the
              claim or rewrite it down to what the document actually supports.
              If you cannot name a source, you have not verified it. Drop it.
    `beat`    filled in afterwards.

WHY. A past script ran 55 beats with ZERO proper nouns and four numbers. Every
fact in it was true, the prose was clean, and it was unwatchable - a physics
lesson about a hypothetical object. Specificity is not decoration on top of a
clear explanation. It IS the interest.

BINDING: at least twenty beats carry a specific. Count them.

### THE HOOK - beat one

Open on the specific wrong thing almost everyone believes, stated as a claim.
Or open on a tiny everyday version of the same mystery, in the third person.

    "Most people think this shot kills the virus. It doesn't."
    "Every lift has a close-door button and none of them do anything."

Never "picture this", "you wake up", "it is the year". Never a table of
contents. If the line would fit unchanged in front of a different explainer in
the same genre, it is a stock intro.

Beat three names the true mechanism and asks the spine question out loud.

KEEP IT ALIVE. Most scripts open adversarially and turn into a neutral
walkthrough by beat four. Every section reopens the gap: what it looks like,
what is really happening, who made it that way.


## 5. WRITING THE BEATS

### EVERY SHORT BEAT MUST CARRY NEW INFORMATION

The most broken rule here. A filler short beat reads fine alone, and arrives
when the beat quota starts to hurt. Real examples from one finished script:

    "The answer begins in the sky."   "That solved one big problem."
    "It worked perfectly at first."   "Then the plane landed again."

Nineteen of that script's 55 beats were like this - a minute of screen time
saying nothing.

TEST: delete the beat, read its neighbours back to back. If nothing is lost, it
was padding. Do not rewrite it. Delete it and spend the beat on a deflation or
a specific that did not make the cut.

A short beat may be a consequence, a reaction, or a deflation drop. Never a
transition, a signpost, a restatement, or a promise that something is coming.

### CAUSATION, NOT CONNECTORS

Between every pair of beats, check SILENTLY that BUT or THEREFORE fits. If only
AND THEN fits, one beat is doing nothing - rewrite it.

DO NOT WRITE THE WORD. This is an editing test, not a sentence template. At
most three beats in the whole script may begin with "But" or "Therefore".

A past run took this literally and opened 53 of 55 beats with one of the two
words, using "Therefore" 32 times in a 521-word script. Worse, gluing the
connector on made a run of nine thesis-restating beats LOOK causally chained
when nothing was happening. The causation belongs in what the beats say.

### LAND ON A PERSON

When a beat says why something is the way it is, the answer is a named human
who wanted something.

    PROCESS: "The standard was adopted for manufacturing efficiency."
    PERSON:  "Stephenson already owned the tooling, so everyone matched him."

"Efficiency", "cost pressures", "practical considerations" are categories with
the person hidden inside. Find the person.

### USE SIMPLE WORDS. THIS IS NOT OPTIONAL.

Write every sentence in the simplest words that carry the meaning. If a
shorter, more common word means the same thing, use the shorter one. Every
time. No exceptions for sounding clever.

The viewer is a fluent adult who learned English as a second language. She
reads and speaks it well but did not grow up with it. She hears this once, at
speed, and cannot rewind. If she has to stop and work out a word, she has
already missed the next sentence.

TEST EVERY WORD: would she know it instantly, on sight, with no pause? If there
is any doubt at all, swap it for the plain one.

ALWAYS THE SHORT TWIN:

    money not economic          city not settlement       food not supplies
    trade not commerce          fell apart not collapsed  showed not demonstrated
    guess not estimate          found not uncovered       about not approximately
    because of not due to       so not consequently       but not however
    most of not the majority of also not furthermore      let not enable

NEVER USE THESE. If a beat contains one, rewrite the sentence:

    systemic, economic, psychological, unavoidable, devastating, catastrophic,
    significant, substantial, fundamental, essentially, effectively, ultimately,
    consequently, furthermore, moreover, thereby, whereby, utilise, facilitate,
    comprise, constitute, in terms of, the concept of, the notion of, a
    testament to, plays a role in, serves as.

CUT THESE, they only fill space:

    completely, totally, absolutely, incredibly, extremely, massively,
    entirely, actually, really.

THE LISTS ARE NOT THE WHOLE RULE. They are only the words that kept coming
back. Read every word you write and apply the test above, even when the fancy
word is on no list here.

THE ONE EXCEPTION: proper nouns, real names, official titles and the technical
term the topic is actually about are NEVER simplified. Explain what it means in
plain words the first time - "oxytocin, the bonding chemical" - and keep the
name. Simplifying names is how a script ends up with none in it.

Simple words for the writing. Never for the facts.

### SCALE ANCHORS

Every number gets a physical comparison or it does not ship.

    WEAK:   "It had very little working memory."
    ANCHOR: "The whole computer held about four kilobytes."
    ANCHOR: "The walls were as thin as three sheets of cooking foil."
    ANCHOR: "Rocks the size of cars waited there."

An abstract ratio is not an anchor. "Millions of times faster" is nothing to
picture; a musical greeting card is.

THIS APPLIES TO QUANTITIES ONLY. Never to a date. A year is not a quantity the
viewer needs a sense of scale for, and anchoring one produces a beat about
something else entirely.

    BANNED: "Galileo was building his first telescope then."
    BANNED: "Boston was just a small wooden village."
    BANNED: "Cell phones were just becoming common back then."

Those are real beats from one script about the history of exorcism. Each one
stops the video to mention an unrelated event on another continent. Three of
them cost ten seconds and broke the frame three times. If a date needs context,
give it in the topic's own terms - what had already happened in THIS story -
or give it none.

### DEAD AND LIVE

    DEAD: "Ancient texts mention conflicts, suggesting warfare happened there."
    LIVE: "Hittite kings wrote letters complaining about a troublesome city in
           exactly that spot."

Concrete nouns, verbs doing the work. Note what the live version has: names.

Read every line aloud once. If you trip, cut the clause. Vary sentence length -
nine words forty times running is a metronome once a voice reads it.


## 6. ACCURACY

Search the web. Do not rely on memory. Prefer primary sources: papers, official
data, museum and university pages, original reporting.

* If you cannot verify it, do not use it. There is no invented-mechanics
  exception in an explainer.
* Say plainly what is disputed or estimated. Name whoever produced an estimate.
* NEVER INVENT CAUSATION. No beat asserts that one fact caused another unless
  that causal link is itself in `facts_to_check` with a source. A past run
  wrote "because the machine was overloaded, it steered them past the safe
  zone" - two true facts joined by a link that does not exist, and it became
  the bridge between two sections. Smoothing the story is where fabrication
  gets in.
* `facts_to_check` is a constraint list, not a disclaimer list. Whatever you
  write in it binds the beats. Read it back before beat one and again after.


## 7. CHECK BY LOOKING

Do not answer these from memory.

* 55 beats; 500-540 words with tags stripped; nothing on 9 words or over 15
* a third to two thirds SHORT; max three SHORT and two LONG in a row
* 5-6 locations, 8+ beats each, every beat actually happens where its label
  says, and no location name contains a date, a year, a decade or an era word
* no narration names the illustrated character or uses "he"/"you" for him
* one beat in three tagged, all from the list, none after
  [whispers]/[excited]/[laughs] left untagged
* no digits, no quote marks, no em dashes in any narration
* READ EVERY WORD of every beat. Not one would make a fluent ESL speaker
  pause to place it. No word from the never-use or space-filler lists appears
  anywhere. The only hard words left are proper nouns and the topic's own
  technical term, each explained in plain words on first use
* at most THREE beats begin with "But" or "Therefore" - count them
* every `deflations` entry: drop_beat = build_beat + 1, build is 10-15 words,
  drop is 4-8, and the `assumed` line is actually SAID by the build beat
* the deflations are four to six DIFFERENT ideas, not one idea restated
* every `specifics.fact` has a number, date or person doing something - no
  bare names; every `source` is a real document whose locator covers that
  exact number
* twenty or more beats carry a specific
* EVERY short beat survives the deletion test, one at a time
* every "why" beat names a person, not a category
* every QUANTITY has a physical scale anchor, and no beat anchors a DATE by
  naming an unrelated event somewhere else in the world
* no beat asserts causation absent from `facts_to_check`
* no beat states what a person or institution WANTED unless a source says so.
  Read every "they wanted / just wanted / the real goal was / in order to"
  and check it against a document. If there is none, rewrite it as what the
  decision did
* for every `specifics` entry, the named source actually contains that exact
  claim - not the surrounding topic, the claim. Check them one at a time
* no joke is an invented comparison. Every aside reacts to a fact that is in
  the script; if the humour comes from a simile you wrote, cut it
* beat one is specific enough that it would not fit another explainer
* the true mechanism is named by beat three
* the wrong assumption is still doing work after beat twenty
* the last five beats make five DIFFERENT points
* the last beat answers the opening question and never says "so that is how
  it works"

Then output the JSON, and nothing else.
